Imagine standing on a clean beach while millions of plastic particles move through the water in front of you. My research follows these particles after storms. Working with three coastal communities, I collect water at river mouths, shallow bays and offshore sites, and because each sample is filtered, chemically identified and compared with rainfall, tide direction, drainage flow and the shape of the coastline, the study can show how several environmental forces combine to move pollution through a connected coastal system that cannot be understood from a single sampling point. We found that the largest pulse arrives within twenty-four hours of heavy rain. The particles then remain trapped in sheltered bays for several days. Current monitoring often samples too late and in the wrong place. Our mobile sampling design captures the short window when pollution is most concentrated. Cleaner-looking water is not necessarily cleaner water. To protect the coast, we need to measure plastic when it moves, not only where it finally settles.
