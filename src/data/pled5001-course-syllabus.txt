THE HONG KONG UNIVERSITY OF SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY (GUANGZHOU) PLED5001 COMMUNICATING RESEARCH IN ENGLISH 1 credit Name: Jaeuk Park Email: jaeukpark@hkust-gz.edu.cn Office Hours: 2-4 Tuesday, E1 312 Course Description This course aims to help research postgraduate students to develop skills they need to understand how to successfully communicate research in English to academic, cross-disciplinary and non-specialist audiences. Students who fail to satisfy the University's English language requirement are advised to complete LANG 5000 before enrolling for this course. Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs) By the end of this course, students should be able to: 1. Describe and apply academic, stylistic, and rhetorical conventions of research text genres. 2. Communicate in speech and in writing critical and analytical interpretations of research texts. 3. Communicate a persuasive research argument and contribution. 4. Express a confident research persona through an engaging style of communication for academic and professional success. 5. Communicate research effectively to academic, cross-disciplinary, and non-specialist audiences. Mapping of Course ILOs to Assessment Tasks Assessed Task Mapped ILOs Explanation Continuous Self-study work ILOs 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 The continuous self- study work assessment evaluates students’ ability to independently engage with and critically analyze research texts through various formats such as analyses, reflections, and pieces of writing. This assessment requires students to apply academic, stylistic, and rhetorical conventions appropriate for different research genres, demonstrating clarity, coherence, and adherence to scholarly standards (ILO 1). Through continuous self-study, students are expected to communicate their critical and analytical interpretations of research texts, showcasing depth of understanding, insight, and the ability to evaluate complex ideas (ILO 2). Additionally, this assessment provides an opportunity for students to articulate persuasive research arguments and highlight their contributions in writing, refining their ability to construct logical and well-supported arguments (ILO 3). It also encourages students to express a confident research persona by developing an engaging and professional writing style, essential for academic and professional success (ILO 4). Furthermore, students will practice communicating their research

findings effectively to diverse audiences, enhancing their ability to make their research accessible and impactful (ILO 5). Assessed written text ILOs 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 The writing assessment focuses on students' ability to describe and apply the academic, stylistic, and rhetorical conventions of research text genres (ILO 1). Through this task, students are expected to demonstrate their skills in communicating critical and analytical interpretations of research texts in writing, showcasing their ability to engage with and analyze complex ideas (ILO 2). The writing assignment also requires students to develop and communicate a persuasive research argument and articulate their contributions to their field of study effectively (ILO 3). Moreover, this assessment encourages students to convey a confident research persona through a clear and engaging written style, which is critical for success in academic and professional contexts (ILO 4). Finally, the writing task allows students to practice tailoring their communication to different audiences, including academic peers, cross-disciplinary readers, and non-specialists, enhancing their ability to make their research accessible and impactful (ILO 5).

PLE Approach to GenAI The PLE embraces innovation and technology in teaching. In line with the wider HKUST approach, the PLE recognises Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) as the way of the future. For this reason, use of GenAI tools such as ChatGPT for academic studies is encouraged, in order to help students maximise their learning potential. The overall approach in the PLE is to rely firmly on pedagogical principles appropriate for the high calibre of university education at HKUST(GZ), along with the commitment to ethics and academic integrity. The principle of ethics is expressed through honesty, clarity, and transparency. As we are all learning to use this new tool, it is essential that everyone – students and teachers alike – takes an approach of complete openness in relation to using GenAI. GenAI and Academic Integrity As mentioned in the above statement, GenAI is a new learning tool and resource, and HKUST(GZ) students are expected to follow the standards as stated in the Academic Honor Code of HKUST(GZ). (Click the link) Specific Guidelines for PLED5001 a) You are NOT allowed to use ChatGPT to create a whole piece of writing. b) You could use ChatGPT to refine your writing in relation to course assessment criteria (e.g., research argument, style, and language); you can use it to brainstorm your writing, create structure, proofread your grammar, and receive feedback. c) You are advised to follow the following recommended procedures. d) You need to submit a GenAI Declaration Form (see below) even if you do not use such tools. Recommended Procedures on Using GenAI for the Assessed Piece of Research Writing Task Step 1 : Draft your research writing. This is your initial draft (Draft 1). Step 2 : Take your draft to GenAI, specifically ChatGPT, for detailed feedback in relation to evaluation criteria (Research Argument, Structure, Style and so forth). Critically evaluate the feedback. Step 3 : Take time to reflect on what you have learned. Based on reflection, make the necessary revisions. This will be your second draft (Draft 2). Step 4 : Following an in-depth reflection, you will further refine your draft. Complete the final draft, ensuring you appropriately reference all sources with in-text citations and acknowledge the usage of GenAI. Use the preferred reference format in your target discipline and include the total word count at the end. You can take this final draft to ChatGPT to create a revised version (Draft 3). Step 5 : The concluding step is to submit the final draft to Canvas by the specified deadline.

Communication and Feedback Assessment results for the Writing tasks will be communicated via Canvas within two weeks of submission. Feedback for the Writing tasks will focus on strengths such as structure, style, argument development, and critical analysis, and will also identify areas for improvement, including language accuracy, coherence, depth of analysis, use of evidence, and overall persuasiveness. Continuous self-study work (e.g., analyses, reflections, pieces of writing) will be monitored online to ensure consistent engagement and progress. While no formal feedback will be provided for self-study activities, students are encouraged to reflect on their learning processes and seek guidance during scheduled office hours if needed. The form and content of feedback for all assessed tasks will be provided at the instructor's discretion, tailored to support each student’s development. Students who have further questions about their feedback or grades on the Writing tasks are encouraged to consult the instructor within five working days of receiving their feedback. Resubmission Policy Once an assignment has been graded, resubmission is not permitted.
